# Hybrid insert optics

The `Visual Pinball/Insert Hybrid` shader combines a Blender bake with a virtual lamp underneath the insert. It is an opaque HDRP Lit material: reflections and shadows use HDRP, while the apparent transmitted light is emitted by the insert itself. It needs neither a screen-space refraction buffer nor a real light below the playfield to show the lamp.

## Authoring

1. Generate an insert and enable **Bake Hybrid Optics** in its inspector. Bake with Blender 5 using the bundled scripts; no insert add-on is required. UVPackMaster remains optional.
2. Click **Use HDRP Hybrid Material** after the bake. This creates a new material and keeps the previous material asset. Undo restores the previous assignment; generated assets stay in the bake folder for reuse or manual cleanup. The button requires the HDRP package and an unshared, fresh bake; a duplicated insert must bake its own data first. Rebaking a duplicate preserves its hybrid shader and tuned parameters in a separate material.
3. Click **Add Intensity Controls and Spill Light**. The new **Insert Light** component exposes **Intensity** (0–1) and **Color** for previewing the lamp in edit mode. Zero intensity is off; one is full output at the material's **Max Brightness**. The **Environment Spill** section sets **Spill Lumens**, **Spill Range** and the light reference. The generated point light sits just above the insert so it can illuminate the playfield and nearby objects; its local position is editable. The initial spill has no shadows for a low-cost glow; enable shadows on the light if surrounding occluders need them.
4. **Virtual Lamp** is local-space XYZ and radius W, all in metres. Its default sits below the mesh. Move it sideways to shift the bright patch, lower it to increase parallax, or increase its radius to soften the moving image. **Bulb Definition** adds a tighter source image inside that halo. All faces refract the same source; their brightness variation does not move or blur the bulb independently. These controls do not require a rebake. The bake's broad scattered-light component stays fixed when the virtual lamp is moved.
5. **Facet Contrast** emphasizes opposite starburst slopes. **Mottle Amount** adds restrained, softly blended cellular variation and small differences between faces. **Mottle Size (mm)** sets the cell spacing (smaller means finer detail), and **Grain** adds a separate fine texture to the illuminated and unlit plastic. **Variation Seed** chooses another pattern. Both textures are fixed in local space and fade out when their detail becomes smaller than a pixel. The bake supplies the actual fan centre and sector count for the face variation. None of these controls needs a rebake.
6. Keep **Edge Glow** and **Surface Normal Strength** low: bevels provide small reflections, while the broader face response carries the pattern. Balance **Baked Scatter** against **Virtual Lamp Strength**; too much scatter or brightness washes out the starburst. **Optical Facet Strength** changes ray distortion independently of surface highlights.

**Facet Flatten** turns the shading normal of the whole body towards the insert's flat top, for the lights and reflection probes only; the baked optical normals that refract the lamp are not affected. Room light enters a real clear insert through that flat top, and the facets underneath only redistribute what is already inside, so with the facets acting as the shading normal a clear insert alternates bright and dark ribs under a key light like a metal grille. Zero keeps the facets, which suits coloured inserts; around 0.85 is right for clear plastic, where the small residual keeps a hint of the ridges. This is a material value and needs no rebake.

The material's **Max Brightness** is an unrestricted multiplier: type values such as 10, 50 or 100 to test stronger illumination directly in the editor. It remains editable with Insert Light attached and sets the output at Intensity 1. The component has no second brightness multiplier. For example, Max Brightness 20 and Intensity 0.5 give ten times the nominal output, for both the lens and spill. Neither control requires a rebake.

**Min Brightness** controls how much of the plastic body's tint remains visible under ambient lighting at Intensity 0. The default, 0.04, makes the off insert substantially darker; zero removes its diffuse colour, and one restores the previous off appearance. This fades to the authored tint as Intensity reaches one. It does not leave the bulb emission or environmental spill on, and the clear cover's specular reflections remain independent.

The material's **Lamp Emission (HDR)** is the nominal radiance, initially 600 per channel. Keep it neutral white and set the lamp colour on the component or through ROM events. This is an exposure-dependent artistic value, not a physical bulb calibration; **Spill Lumens** independently calibrates the actual light's output with Max Brightness and Intensity at one. Material Max Brightness uses `_InsertLuminosity` and is never overridden by the lamp controller.

The geometry and baked atlas remain those of the insert generator. Rebaking updates all three textures together, including optics whenever the current baked material uses them. It preserves the material's tuned parameters. Geometry edits invalidate the bake but keep the current material as a preview. An inspector warning asks you to bake again, since the old textures may no longer align with the new geometry. Until then, the mesh uses its generated UVs and freshly calculated tangents; stale baked vertex data is never reused. **Clear Bake** restores the original unbaked material.

## Runtime RGB

Place the renderer on or below a VPE `LightComponent`, as with other emissive lamp meshes. VPE drives `_InsertLampLevel` and `_InsertLampColor` through a `MaterialPropertyBlock`, without changing the shared material or any textures. The RGB property is a linear vector; VPE converts incoming Unity `Color` values with `.linear`. Colour events do not change the current level, fading does not reset the last colour, and an off insert keeps its visible reflective plastic surface. At startup, the insert component supplies the initial colour, so ordinary on/off ROM lamps keep their configured colour until an RGB event changes it. Lamps without an insert component use an ordinary child Unity light or white.

Map the parent VPE Light to its ROM lamp in Lamp Manager. One VPE Light represents one colour: all inserts and native lights beneath it share that colour; use separate VPE Lights for independently coloured inserts. Its lamp API drives intensity, fades and RGB; the component's Intensity and Color fields reflect those values during play. `InsertLightComponent` follows the renderer state in `LateUpdate` and applies it to the real spill light, scaled by the material's Max Brightness. VPE excludes this managed light from its independent native-light cache so there is only one owner. Lamp Manager previews also update the component in edit mode. Removing the hybrid material disables the spill. Without a parent VPE Light, the component's Intensity and Color control it at runtime as well as in the editor. The virtual lamp remains an image within the insert; the real point light supplies environmental illumination. Ordinary emissive HDRP material behaviour and any enabled SSGI still apply.

## Clear cover

Assign any material under **Clear Cover → Material** to close the insert at its rim with a flat top in the second material slot. This is the default workflow: the cover uses local 0–1 UVs and the assigned material's settings, with no playfield dependency or material overrides. Clear the Material to remove the top surface. **Add Clear Cover** creates a nearly transparent **Visual Pinball/Insert Cover** material as a starting point; its **Normal Map**, tiling/offset, **Normal Strength**, **Smoothness** and **Opacity** are editable. Custom materials control their own transparency, reflections and shadows.

Enable **Use Playfield Normals** on an Insert Cover material for continuous surface detail across the playfield and insert. It automatically finds the surface under the enclosing Playfield (or the single Playfield in the same Table), and reads its first material's normal map. Imported boards are selected from readable planar surfaces with normal maps, preferring the largest; **Playfield Override** can select a different surface. The inspector shows the detected renderer, source material and source normal map. Nothing needs to be copied into the cover material manually, and its shared material asset is not modified.

The cover derives the actual XZ-to-UV0 mapping from the source mesh's first submesh, including margins, rotation and flips, and follows the source base-map tiling, offset and normal strength. The source must have Read/Write enabled and planar top-surface UV0; HDRP Lit tangent-space normal maps are supported. Moving, rotating or scaling either surface updates the cover's UVs and tangents in edit mode and at runtime. Disabling the option, or losing the source normal map, restores local UVs and the cover's own material settings. Unsupported sources show an inspector warning. Material smoothness remains independent: match it to the playfield's mask-map smoothness for a continuous highlight. The source normal map must contain detail over the insert area.

The built-in cover defaults to **Opacity** 0.01 and **Smoothness** 0.96, preserving HDRP specular reflections at low opacity. **Normal Strength** multiplies the playfield's normal strength when that option is enabled; one matches it. Otherwise it controls the cover material's own normal map. The built-in cover does not emit light or cast a solid shadow and keeps the refracted virtual bulb visible without screen-space refraction.

The body's atlas, tangents and bake stay intact; adding or moving the cover needs no rebake. Geometry changes still require rebaking the body as usual. If Blender's unwrap folds triangles, the baker restores and packs the generator's layout instead, validating that no two surfaces share a texel before baking.

## Bake data

| Asset | Encoding | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal Map | Existing tangent-space normal bake | HDRP reflections, with adjustable strength |
| Optical Normals | Linear RGB = sharp local normal × 0.5 + 0.5 | Optical direction without rounded creases, independent of atlas rotation |
| Optical Response | Linear R: normalized cavity irradiance; G: rounded-edge response; B: transmission mask | Broad internal light, ridge/rim glow and view-dependent lamp weighting |

The optical maps use the same regular/flat projections, UVs and padding as the normal bake. Blender illuminates a white diffuse cavity proxy from above, bakes direct and indirect light, normalizes it and lightly filters the low-frequency result. It separately bakes sharp optical normals and bevel edge angles. The existing surface normal bake retains its bevels for reflections. All optical coefficients are neutral: lamp hue is applied only in Unity, so RGB changes never require a new bake. The two data textures import with sRGB and CPU readability disabled, clamping and no compression. Older hybrid bakes need one rebake for sharp optical normals and the fan metadata.

The shader refracts the object-space view direction through the baked optical normal and intersects a virtual lamp plane. A tight source inside a soft halo approximates the refracted bulb, with another broad lobe for an internal reflected image. View position is evaluated per fragment and per eye. It does not sample the screen, so the lamp remains available off-screen and behind opaque playfield geometry.

## Limits

This is an artistic light-transport approximation. The body bake uses an open tray even when the optional clear cover is enabled, so the bake is not transmission through a closed plastic solid, and the runtime lobe is not a simulated caustic. There is no printed insert artwork layer, real thickness-dependent absorption, or full internal reflection solution. The body's optical controls work best at ordinary playfield viewing angles and with a uniformly scaled insert. A more physical bake would first need a closed optical source mesh.

The extra shader cost over Lit includes two texture samples, a refracted direction, analytic lamp evaluations, procedural facet variation, a nine-cell soft cellular pattern and fine value noise. Each enabled spill is also a real point light. The optical maps add texture memory; 1024² uncompressed RGBA textures with mipmaps take roughly 5.3 MiB each. Atlas packing and lower bake resolutions are useful when a table has many distinct inserts.
