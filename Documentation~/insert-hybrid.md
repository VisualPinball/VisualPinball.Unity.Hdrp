# Hybrid insert optics

The `Visual Pinball/Insert Hybrid` shader combines a Blender bake with a virtual lamp underneath the insert. It is an opaque HDRP Lit material: reflections and shadows use HDRP, while the apparent transmitted light is emitted by the insert itself. It needs neither a screen-space refraction buffer nor a real light below the playfield to show the lamp.

## Authoring

1. Generate an insert and enable **Bake Hybrid Optics** in its inspector. Bake with Blender 5 using the bundled scripts; no insert add-on is required. UVPackMaster remains optional.
2. Click **Use HDRP Hybrid Material** after the bake. This creates a new material and keeps the previous material asset. Undo restores the previous assignment; generated assets stay in the bake folder for reuse or manual cleanup. The button requires the HDRP package and an unshared, fresh bake; a duplicated insert must bake its own data first. Rebaking a duplicate preserves its hybrid shader and tuned parameters in a separate material.
3. Click **Add Intensity Controls and Spill Light**. The new **Insert Light** component exposes **Intensity** (0–1) and **Color** for previewing the lamp in edit mode. Zero intensity is off; one is full output at the material's **Max Brightness**. The **Environment Spill** section sets **Spill Lumens**, **Spill Range** and the light reference. The generated spill is a wide spot light just under the insert, pointing up through it at the ball and whatever else is above; its transform is editable. The initial spill has no shadows for a low-cost glow; enable shadows on the light if surrounding occluders need them.
4. The material's inspector groups the controls into **Plastic** (the body as room light shows it), **Lamp** (the virtual lamp, its glow and the facets) and **Baked Data** (the maps the bake wrote, shown but not edited there), between HDRP's usual surface and advanced options. Every control has a tooltip, and controls the shader would ignore in the current state are greyed out: the cavity controls without Clarity, Min Brightness at full Clarity, the facet controls when the bake has no starburst, the lamp at zero Max Brightness except for what a clear body's cavity shares with it. **Virtual Lamp** is a position and radius in insert space, edited in millimetres (the shader property `_OpticalLamp` holds metres). Its default sits below the mesh. Move it sideways to shift the bright patch, lower it to increase parallax, or increase its radius to soften the moving image. The view ray is traced into the plastic through the flat top and out through the facet into the cavity, so the bulb shows its full depth: it shifts against the body as the eye moves, and each eye of a headset sees it in its own place. That is the halo, **Virtual Lamp Strength** and **Bulb Definition**; **Baked Scatter** is the light the whole body scatters and does not move, so a body that is all scatter, or so bright it clips to white, shows no parallax in the bulb's image; a clear body's cavity look-through follows the same ray and keeps moving. **Bulbs** raises that to up to four virtual lamps, for a banana with its bulbs in a row: each has a position of its own (`_OpticalLampB` to `_OpticalLampD`, off while their radius is zero), the radius is shared, and every bulb adds its own halo and its own share of the baked glow, each fading with its own Lamp Spread, so one bulb lights its end of a banana and fades along the body, the bulbs beside it that are off taking nothing away, and two bulbs on add up where they meet. Max Brightness and the emission colour are shared; each bulb has a level and colour of its own, the first the Insert Light's Intensity and Color, the others its **Bulb 2** to **Bulb 4** rows, which the inspector shows for the bulbs the material has. Each bulb's halo carries its own colour, and the baked glow is the sum of every bulb's light, colour and all, so a bulb fading in colours every point it reaches in proportion to its level rather than its colour creeping in from the bulb. **Tint With Lamp** follows the same mix, on the body and on the cover, and reaches as far as the light does, whatever the intensity: a bulb fading in tints its whole reach at once and brightens within it, instead of its colour creeping outwards with the light. Between off and a tenth of full the tint fades in place. A banana lit red, green and blue along its length is hazed red, green and blue, since the Insert Light hands the bulbs' positions and drives to the cover's property block as well. Each bulb is a lamp of its own in Lamp Manager: an insert with more than one bulb offers its bulbs in the Element column, by the names given them on the Insert Light, such as Left, Middle and Right, or **Bulb 1** to **Bulb 4** where they have none; the Insert Light shows each bulb's position beside its name, so the right lamp goes to the right bulb. Each takes its own mapping, RGB channels included, so a banana's three lamps drive its three bulbs; a mapping made before the bulbs came drives the first. Only the first bulb blinks; a blinking status leaves the others on. **Bulb Definition** adds a tighter source image inside that halo. All faces refract the same source; their brightness variation does not move or blur the bulb independently. These controls do not require a rebake. The bake's broad scattered-light component stays fixed when the virtual lamp is moved, unless **Lamp Spread** is set: then the whole lit look dims with the distance across the insert from the lamp's position, and moves with it.
5. **Facet Contrast** emphasizes opposite starburst slopes. **Mottle Amount** adds restrained, softly blended cellular variation and small differences between faces. **Mottle Size (mm)** sets the cell spacing (smaller means finer detail), and **Grain** adds a separate fine texture to the illuminated and unlit plastic. **Variation Seed** chooses another pattern. Both textures are fixed in local space and fade out when their detail becomes smaller than a pixel. The bake supplies the actual fan centre and sector count for the face variation. None of these controls needs a rebake.
6. Keep **Edge Glow** and **Surface Normal Strength** low: bevels provide small reflections, while the broader face response carries the pattern. Balance **Baked Scatter** against **Virtual Lamp Strength**; too much scatter or brightness washes out the starburst. **Lamp Spread (mm)** is how far from each virtual bulb the lit look, halo and glow alike, reaches before it has fallen to half, to a fifth at twice that and a tenth at three times, the way light spreads from a point: a real insert is brightest around its bulb and dims away from it, so a spread of about a third of the insert's length keeps Max Brightness from flooding the whole body while one bulb still shows at the far end of a banana. Zero, the default, lights the whole body alike. **Optical Facet Strength** changes ray distortion independently of surface highlights.

**Facet Flatten** turns the shading normal of the whole body towards the insert's flat top, for the lights and reflection probes only; the baked optical normals that refract the lamp are not affected. Room light enters a real clear insert through that flat top, and the facets underneath only redistribute what is already inside, so with the facets acting as the shading normal a clear insert alternates bright and dark ribs under a key light like a metal grille. Zero keeps the facets, which suits coloured inserts; around 0.85 is right for clear plastic, where the small residual keeps a hint of the ridges. This is a material value and needs no rebake.

A **material template** carries the look of the hybrid material and the clear cover, everything shown in their inspectors but the baked maps and the virtual lamps, so a clear insert and a coloured one are one template apart. The insert's **Material Template** section puts a template on the selected inserts' materials, and saves the look of an insert as a new one; **Clear** and **Colored** ship with the package. A material shared by several inserts changes for all of them.

**Clarity** switches the unlit body from tinted plastic to clear plastic. Clear plastic absorbs nothing, so an off clear insert shows the lamp board or reflector under it, lit by room light that entered through the top; that is why a real clear insert is nearly as bright as the white artwork around it, lamp or no lamp. At Clarity one the body's albedo is **Cavity Color** instead of the tint scaled by Min Brightness, and each starburst face shows the part of that cavity its refracted view ray lands on: a wide, soft falloff around the virtual lamp, so the ribs shift gently with the viewpoint instead of alternating under the lights. **Cavity Spread** is that falloff in virtual lamp radii, and **Cavity Contrast** how much darker a face looking past the cavity gets; keep it around 0.3 for the low-contrast ribs of a real clear insert. Walls and the channel take the baked cavity irradiance. The tint still multiplies, so a lightly tinted clear insert keeps its colour. Zero, the default, leaves coloured inserts exactly as before. None of this needs a rebake.

**Normal Grain Strength** and **Normal Grain Size (VPX)** in the component's **Normal Bake** section add fine surface texture to the baked tangent-space normal map. New inserts default to strength 0.25 and size 1.5 VPX units (about 0.8 mm); set strength to zero for a smooth surface. Change these settings and **Bake Again** to update the reflections. The grain is continuous in insert-local space, independent of UV packing, and does not change the optical bulb normals, cavity illumination or clear cover. The material's existing **Grain** control affects colour and emission separately.

The material's **Max Brightness** is an unrestricted multiplier: type values such as 10, 50 or 100 to test stronger illumination directly in the editor. It remains editable with Insert Light attached and sets the output at Intensity 1. The component has no second brightness multiplier. For example, Max Brightness 20 and Intensity 0.5 give ten times the nominal output, for both the lens and spill. Neither control requires a rebake.

**Min Brightness** controls how much of the plastic body's tint remains visible under ambient lighting at Intensity 0. The default, 0.04, makes the off insert substantially darker; zero removes its diffuse colour, and one restores the previous off appearance. This fades to the authored tint as Intensity reaches one. It does not leave the bulb emission or environmental spill on, and the clear cover's specular reflections remain independent.

The material's **Lamp Emission (HDR)** is the nominal radiance, initially 600 per channel. Keep it neutral white and set the lamp colour on the component or through ROM events. This is an exposure-dependent artistic value, not a physical bulb calibration; **Spill Lumens** independently calibrates the actual light's output with Max Brightness and Intensity at one. Material Max Brightness uses `_InsertLuminosity` and is never overridden by the lamp controller.

The geometry and baked atlas remain those of the insert generator. Rebaking updates all three textures together, including optics whenever the current baked material uses them. It preserves the material's tuned parameters. Geometry edits invalidate the bake but keep the current material on. Its maps no longer lie on the new geometry, so until the next bake the insert shows its geometry instead: the mesh's own shading normal, with **Facet Flatten**, **Surface Normal Strength**, **Optical Facet Strength** and **Facet Contrast** held at zero in the renderer's property block, nothing written to the material, and the inspector says so. An engraved line is geometry for the bake alone: the bake keeps the middle's chart as the generator projected it from above, and while the bake fits the middle's triangles, trough and all, give way to a plain flat island carrying the maps; change the line and its trough is back in the mesh, shown as geometry, so it can be tuned step by step and baked once. A bake made before the middle kept its projection leaves the trough in the mesh and the inspector asks for one more bake. Baking again lifts the hold. The mesh uses its generated UVs and freshly calculated tangents while the bake is stale; stale baked vertex data is never reused. **Clear Bake** restores the original unbaked material.

## Runtime RGB

Select the **Insert Light** component as the destination in **Lamp Manager**. No parent Light component is required. Use **Single On/Off** for a binary ROM lamp, **Single Fading** with the ROM's fading-step count for modulated brightness, or **RGB** for colour plus intensity. For separate RGB outputs, map all three ROM IDs to the same Insert Light using **RGB Multi** and select the red, green and blue channel respectively; an optional fourth alpha channel controls intensity. The component is also available in Light Groups and as a wire destination. Lamp Manager's on/off previews work in edit mode.

VPE applies normalized ROM intensity and colour through a material property block on the body's material slot. It never changes the shared material, the clear cover, or the material's **Max Brightness**. Positive fading values turn on a previously off insert; colour-only changes preserve its current intensity, including zero. RGB channel changes retain the other channels and update the spill light together with the lens. The plastic remains visible while its lamp is off.

Existing inserts under a VPE **LightComponent** can still follow that parent when they are not mapped directly. Once an Insert Light receives its own lamp or wire events, those events take precedence over the parent. Without a mapping or parent, the component's Intensity and Color remain available for manual control. The virtual lamp is an image within the insert; the real spot light under it supplies the illumination of what is above. Ordinary emissive HDRP material behaviour and any enabled SSGI still apply.

## Clear cover

Assign any material under **Clear Cover → Material** to close the insert at its rim with a flat top in the second material slot. This is the default workflow: the cover uses local 0–1 UVs and the assigned material's settings, with no playfield dependency or material overrides. Clear the Material to remove the top surface. **Add Clear Cover** creates a nearly transparent **Visual Pinball/Insert Cover** material as a starting point; its **Normal Map**, tiling/offset, **Normal Strength**, **Smoothness** and **Opacity** are editable. Custom materials control their own transparency, reflections and shadows.

Enable **Use Playfield Normals** on an Insert Cover material for continuous surface detail across the playfield and insert. It automatically finds the surface under the enclosing Playfield (or the single Playfield in the same Table), and reads its first material's normal map. Imported boards are selected from readable planar surfaces with normal maps, preferring the largest; **Playfield Override** can select a different surface. The inspector shows the detected renderer, source material and source normal map. Nothing needs to be copied into the cover material manually, and its shared material asset is not modified.

The cover derives the actual XZ-to-UV0 mapping from the source mesh's first submesh, including margins, rotation and flips, and follows the source base-map tiling, offset and normal strength. The source must have Read/Write enabled and planar top-surface UV0; HDRP Lit tangent-space normal maps are supported. Moving, rotating or scaling either surface updates the cover's UVs and tangents in edit mode and at runtime. Disabling the option, or losing the source normal map, restores local UVs and the cover's own material settings. Unsupported sources show an inspector warning. Material smoothness remains independent: match it to the playfield's mask-map smoothness for a continuous highlight. The source normal map must contain detail over the insert area.

The cover material's inspector has two sections between HDRP's surface and advanced options, **Haze** and **Surface**, with tooltips on every control; Haze Color is greyed out at zero Opacity, the tiling without a normal map, and a note says how many inserts in the open scenes take the normal map from their playfield instead. The built-in cover defaults to **Opacity** 0.01 and **Smoothness** 0.96, preserving HDRP specular reflections at low opacity. **Haze Color** is what the cover blends in at that opacity, a light grey by default: raising Opacity to somewhere between 0.05 and 0.1 lays a milky veil over the body that softens the ribs the way a worn, slightly frosted top does, which suits clear inserts in particular. **Normal Strength** multiplies the playfield's normal strength when that option is enabled; one matches it. Otherwise it controls the cover material's own normal map. The built-in cover does not emit light or cast a solid shadow and keeps the refracted virtual bulb visible without screen-space refraction.

A grey haze over a coloured lamp makes its colour milky. The Insert Light's **spill** is a real light driven with the lamp: a wide spot, 147 degrees with a soft inner cone, a 2 mm source, no shadows, no bounce and no volumetrics, sitting just under the insert and pointing up through it at the ball and whatever else is above. A spot is cheaper than the point light earlier inserts got and can be aimed; **Add Spill Light** makes one, and an Insert Light that still has a point light offers **Make It a Spot Light**, which turns it round and gives it the spot's range and lumens where it still had the point light's defaults.

**Tint With Lamp** on the Insert Light turns the hue and saturation of the haze, and of the body under it, towards the lamp's while the lamp is lit, by **Tint Amount** at full intensity and in proportion below it, never their brightness: a grey haze comes out the lamp's colour, as bright as the grey was, and a lamp that is off, or a white one, leaves both their own colour. The light writes the lamp's level, colour and tint amount into the cover slot's property block, alongside the playfield-normal projection; the cover material itself stays as it is.

The body's atlas, tangents and bake stay intact; adding or moving the cover needs no rebake. Geometry changes still require rebaking the body as usual. If Blender's unwrap folds triangles, the baker restores and packs the generator's layout instead, validating that no two surfaces share a texel before baking.

## Bake data

| Asset | Encoding | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal Map | Existing tangent-space normal bake | HDRP reflections, with adjustable strength |
| Optical Normals | Linear RGB = sharp local normal × 0.5 + 0.5 | Optical direction without rounded creases, independent of atlas rotation |
| Optical Response | Linear R: normalized cavity irradiance; G: rounded-edge response; B: transmission mask | Broad internal light, ridge/rim glow and view-dependent lamp weighting |

The optical maps use the same regular/flat projections, UVs and padding as the normal bake. Blender illuminates a white diffuse cavity proxy from above, bakes direct and indirect light, normalizes it and lightly filters the low-frequency result. It separately bakes sharp optical normals and bevel edge angles. The existing surface normal bake retains its bevels for reflections. All optical coefficients are neutral: lamp hue is applied only in Unity, so RGB changes never require a new bake. The two data textures import with sRGB and CPU readability disabled, clamping and no compression. Older hybrid bakes need one rebake for sharp optical normals and the fan metadata.

The shader refracts the object-space view direction through the baked optical normal and intersects a virtual lamp plane. A tight source inside a soft halo approximates the refracted bulb, with another broad lobe for an internal reflected image. View position is evaluated per fragment and per eye. It does not sample the screen, so the lamp remains available off-screen and behind opaque playfield geometry.

## Limits

This is an artistic light-transport approximation. The body bake uses an open tray even when the optional clear cover is enabled, so the bake is not transmission through a closed plastic solid, and the runtime lobe is not a simulated caustic. There is no printed insert artwork layer, real thickness-dependent absorption, or full internal reflection solution. The body's optical controls work best at ordinary playfield viewing angles and with a uniformly scaled insert. A more physical bake would first need a closed optical source mesh.

The extra shader cost over Lit includes two texture samples, a refracted direction, analytic lamp evaluations, procedural facet variation, a nine-cell soft cellular pattern and fine value noise. Each enabled spill is also a real spot light. The optical maps add texture memory; 1024² uncompressed RGBA textures with mipmaps take roughly 5.3 MiB each. Atlas packing and lower bake resolutions are useful when a table has many distinct inserts.
